Abstract—Transmitting multimedia files such as audio is a challenging task in secure wireless communication systems. Therefore, encryption methods are required to secure audio content. This work suggests an audio encryption method that gives an effective security level. To achieve this, the pre-distributed key is processed through Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) in Counter (CTR) mode to generate the corresponding subkeys for the audio data blocks. Each subkey is divided into two parts, where the first part is XORed with the information bits containing the audio data, while the remaining part is assigned to the frozen bits. The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed method performs well and is effective against various types of attacks. 
Keywords—Audio encryption, Polar codess, Information bits, Frozen bits, Secret key, Wireless channel characteristics.
Introduction 
The security of audio signal plays an important role in 5G networks, as audio signals not only facilitate convenient information transmission and communication but also create potential opportunities for criminal misuse [1]. The encryption methods are employed to protect the audio signals. However, audio signal is more difficult to encrypt than other types of data since there is the association between adjacent samples. Various audio encryption methods have been proposed, such as mathematical-based approaches [2]–[4], chaos-based schemes [5]–[11], or chaotic maps combined with DNA [12]–[15]. Mathematical-based audio encryption methods typically exhibit weak security due to their deterministic structure, limited randomness, and vulnerability to statistical and cryptanalytic attacks. Chaos-based audio encryption schemes provide high sensitivity and randomness. However, they often suffer from parameter instability and limited cryptographic rigor. Many of these methods are vulnerable to known-plaintext and differential attacks if not carefully designed. Moreover, their computational complexity can be relatively high. Although DNA-based approaches introduce additional randomness, they often incur high complexity and lack strong theoretical security guarantees. In addition, their practical implementation is relatively challenging.
In recent years, crypto-coding technique has emerged as one of the physical-layer security approaches that has attracted considerable research attention, as it enables joint data encryption and channel coding to be performed in a single step. Crypto-coding technique can be implemented on Turbo codes [16]–[19] and polar codess [20]–[27]. However, crypto-coding techniques based on polar codess offer more significant advantages, as polar codess are currently adopted in fifth generation (5G) networks. These crypto-coding based polar codess use a secret key generated from the characteristics of the wireless channel to manipulate the information bits and frozen bits. The security effectiveness of these methods is only validated through evaluating their resistance to passive eavesdropping attacks, while neglecting their capability to encrypt different types of data and their robustness against other active attacks. Moreover, using a single secret key generated from the characteristics of the wireless channel to encrypt all data blocks may compromise security if the system is subjected to active attacks.
This study proposes an audio encryption method based on crypto-coding with polar codess. The proposed method is implemented by using a pre-distributed key to derive subkeys corresponding to the input audio blocks through the Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) key derivation function in Counter (CTR) mode. Each subkey is divided into two parts corresponding to the information bits and the frozen bits. The first part of the subkey is XORed with the audio data embedded in the information bits, while the second part is assigned to the frozen bits. The simulation results show that the proposed method encrypts/decrypts well and is highly effective in resisting attacks on encrypted audio files. Several contributions are summarized as follows:
The AES key derivation function in CTR mode is incorporated to enhance the security of the secret key instead of relying on a single fixed key.
The audio data contained in the information bits are protected using one part of the subkey, while the remaining part is stored in the frozen bits to enhance diffusion and confusion. This method not only improves audio encryption performance but also resists passive eavesdropping attacks and ensures the reliability of the audio data transmitted over wireless channels.

Simulation results
This section presents a detailed performance evaluation of the proposed audio encryption scheme through simulations conducted in MATLAB. The experimental dataset consists of uncompressed ‘.wav‘ audio files, including both mono and stereo signals, with a sampling frequency of Fs = 44.1 kHz and a 16-bit resolution
A. Visual Analysis
To verify the effectiveness of structure destruction in the original signal, we conduct a visual evaluation from three perspectives: the time domain (Waveform), the frequency domain (Spectrogram) và and the amplitude distribution (Histogram) for both mono and stereo audio signals..
 Waveform Analysis:Fig. 3 illustrates the waveform of the original audio signal before and after encryption. It can be observed that the original signal exhibits clear amplitude variations depending on the information content. In contrast, the encrypted signal shows a noise-like distribution within the range [-1,1], completely concealing the amplitude characteristics of the original signal..
 Spectrogram Analysis: Fig. 4 compares the energy spectra of mono and dual-channel audio signals before and after encoding. The original audio signals exhibit energy concentration mainly in the low- and mid-frequency bands, with distinct spectral structures that reflect their underlying information content. After encoding, the spectral energy is uniformly distributed across the entire bandwidth from 0 to 22 kHz.  Histogram Analysis: Fig. 5  shows the probability distribution of the signal amplitude. The histogram of the original signal follows a Gaussian distribution (centered around 0). After applying the proposed algorithm, the probability distribution becomes a uniform distribution. This result demonstrates the algorithm's excellent resistance to statistical attacks.

C. Chosen/Known Plaintext Analysis
Known Plaintext Attacks (KPA) and Chosen-Plaintext Attacks (CPA) typically rely on discovering linear relationships or deterministic patterns between the plaintext and the ciphertext in order to infer the secret key. The proposed method effectively resists these types of attacks, as demonstrated through both theoretical analysis and experimental evaluation. Theoretically, the system employs AES in CTR mode to generate the keystream. Owing to the nonlinear nature of AES and the use of a varying IV, each data block is encrypted with a unique subkey. Consequently, deriving the original secret key from plaintext–ciphertext pairs is computationally impossible. Experimentally, we perform simulations of two hypothetical attack scenarios to verify the security of the proposed scheme.
    KPA scenario: It is assumed that the attacker knows the plaintext of the “Animal” audio file and extracts the corresponding keystream (Key A). The attacker then reuses this keystream to decrypt another file, “Drum” (encrypted with Key B). The results show that the two keystreams are completely different. The decryption error map in  Fig . 9  indicates a Bit Error Rate (BER) of 49.56%, meaning the decrypted output is essentially random noise.

BER values close to 50% in both  scenarios confirm that the system achieves strong confusion and diffusion properties. Even a minor change in the plaintext or the key-generation parameters (IV/Counter) leads to a completely different keystream, effectively preventing any reuse of information from KPA/CPA attacks.
In the cryptanalysis at [32] demonstrates that the secret key can be fully recovered, which is equivalent to the BER dropping to 0% (a total break) and exposing the original audio. In contrast, our proposed scheme maintains a BER of 50.08% under similar CPA conditions (Fig. 10), proving that the attacker recovers nothing but random noise. These results confirm that the suggested approach successfully addresses the structural flaws of chaotic key-chaining, offering enhanced cryptographic robustness to thwart chosen-plaintext analysis.

Conclusion
This paper proposes an audio encryption method based on the Polar encryption algorithm combined with the AES algorithm operating in CTR mode to ensure the reliability and security of data transmission in secure wireless communication systems. The proposed system employs the AES-CTR key generation function to generate dynamic subkeys for each data block, thereby overcoming the drawbacks of fixed-key usage and enhancing security at the physical layer. MATLAB simulation results demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method, with information entropy close to the ideal value (7.9989), a correlation coefficient close to zero, sensitivity metrics meeting security standards (NPCR ≈ 99.61%, UACI ≈ 33.48%), and a low PSNR value. These results indicate that the proposed method is highly effective for audio encryption and provides strong protection against various attacks. The method is suitable for implementation in 5G wireless communication systems and real-time multimedia security applications. In future work, the proposed approach will be deployed on hardware architectures, and proactive attacks will be evaluated to validate its practical feasibility.
